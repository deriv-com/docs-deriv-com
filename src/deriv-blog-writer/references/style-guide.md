# Deriv Blog Style Guide

Incorporates the Deriv Content Style Guide 2025 and blog-specific voice/tone rules.

## The voice in one line

Knowledgeable trading peer — not a salesperson, not a textbook.

## Deriv's 5 tone-of-voice pillars

1. **Clear and direct** — straightforward, jargon-free language; convey core messages efficiently
2. **Friendly and approachable** — warm, inviting, supportive; fosters community
3. **Client-centred** — content centres on the reader's needs and interests
4. **Trustworthy and transparent** — honest about processes, risks, and guidelines
5. **Informative and engaging** — educates and improves understanding; invites interaction

## Core writing principles

**Plain language (global audience rule).** Deriv clients are global; many aren't native English speakers, and content may be translated. Use the simplest word that works.
- ✅ "Synthetic Indices have no exchange hours. They run 24/7, including weekends."
- ❌ "You might be wondering — can you trade on weekends? Great news! With Synthetic Indices, the answer is yes!"

**Active voice, always.**
- ✅ "Set a take-profit level to lock in your gains automatically."
- ❌ "A take-profit level can be set by the trader in order to lock in gains."

**Write positively, not negatively.**
- ✅ "Volatility Indices are available in selected countries."
- ❌ "Volatility Indices are not available in some countries."

**Informative, not promotional.** Explain what the product does and why it matters to the trader. Don't sell.
- ✅ "Multipliers let you increase your market exposure without increasing your maximum loss beyond your initial stake."
- ❌ "Multipliers are an amazing way to supercharge your trading and maximise your potential!"

**Avoid redundancy, ambiguity, and fillers.**
- No redundant phrases: "new innovation", "future plans", "prior experience"
- Be specific: "We'll respond in two days" not "We'll respond soon"
- No filler words: "furthermore", "however", "therefore" — cut them

## English standard: British English throughout

| British (✅ use) | American (❌ avoid) |
|---|---|
| practise (verb) | practice (verb) |
| programme | program |
| colour, centre, authorise | color, center, authorize |
| licence (noun) | license (noun) |

## Grammar & punctuation

**Headlines and subheadings**
- Sentence case throughout (not Title Case) — only capitalise the first word and proper nouns.
  - ✅ "How multipliers work on Deriv Trader"
  - ❌ "How Multipliers Work On Deriv Trader"
- No full stop/period at the end of headlines or subheadings.

**Serial (Oxford) comma** — always use when listing three or more items.
- ✅ "Forex, commodities, and Synthetic Indices"
- ❌ "Forex, commodities and Synthetic Indices"

**Numbers**
- Spell out one through nine; use numerals for 10 and above.
- Exception: always use numerals for percentages (5%, not five per cent). Use the % symbol.
- Numbers over three digits use commas: 1,200 / 4,637,900.
- Exception: leverage and index names don't use commas — "1:1000 leverage", "Crash 1000 Index".
- If a number begins a sentence, spell it out (or rephrase).

**Abbreviations and acronyms**
- Spell out in full on first use, then abbreviate: "contracts for difference (CFDs)... CFD trading allows..."
- No apostrophes to pluralise: CFDs not CFD's; 1990s not 1990's.

**Hyperlinks**
- Never use "click here". Link the noun — descriptive link text.
- ✅ "Visit the Deriv Help centre."
- ❌ "Click here for more information."

**Dates and times (blog articles)**
- Date format: DD Month YYYY (e.g. 22 January 2025) — full form preferred.
- Time format: 12-hour with am/pm — "9:00 am (GMT)". Always include GMT when referencing specific times.

## Banned words & phrases

| Don't use | Use instead |
|---|---|
| invest | trade |
| investor | trader |
| win | earn / receive |
| stake | initial capital |
| amazing, incredible, revolutionary, game-changing | (just describe what it does) |
| easy, simple, just, simply | (omit — or explain why it's accessible) |
| guaranteed, safe investment | (never — compliance risk) |
| "click here" | descriptive link text |
| "furthermore", "however", "therefore" | (cut or rewrite) |
| "as mentioned above" | (restate the point) |
| "in conclusion" | (use a CTA section instead) |
| "leverage your potential", "unlock your trading" | (generic clichés — avoid) |

## Platform & product name rules

Always use exact names — these are proper nouns.

| ✅ Use | ❌ Never use |
|---|---|
| Deriv MT5 | dMT5, Mt5, mt5 |
| Deriv cTrader | Dctrader, DCTRADER |
| Deriv X | DX, DerX |
| Deriv GO | Deriv go, Deriv Go |
| Deriv Bot | dBot, DerivBot |
| Deriv Trader | DTrader, dTrader, DT |
| SmartTrader | ST, smart trader, Smart Trader |

- **Trade type names — capitalised:** Multipliers, Accumulator Options, Vanilla Options.
- **Market names — capitalised:** Synthetic Indices, Derived Indices, Volatility Indices, Crash 1000 Index.

## Tone by section

| Section | Tone |
|---|---|
| Opening paragraph | Neutral, direct — answers the headline immediately |
| Key Takeaways | Concise, factual, standalone value |
| Body sections | Explanatory, calm, concrete examples |
| Comparison tables | Neutral, objective |
| Risk management sections | Serious, honest — never minimise risk |
| CTA | Encouraging but measured — always demo account first |
| FAQ | Conversational but accurate |
| Disclaimer | Formal, verbatim |

## Handling risk language

Never soften or minimise risk — this has compliance implications.

- Always acknowledge trading involves risk when discussing strategies or products.
- Risk management features *manage* risk — they don't eliminate it.
- The standard disclaimer must appear at the end of every article, verbatim.
- Regional disclaimers (EU, UK restrictions) must be included where products have limitations.

**Good:** "Stop-loss helps you define the maximum you're willing to lose on a trade, but doesn't guarantee that outcome in all market conditions."
**Bad:** "Stop-loss keeps your funds safe."
