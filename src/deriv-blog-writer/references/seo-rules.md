# SEO & AI Visibility Rules

Sourced from Deriv SEO Team guidelines for long-form content. These rules apply to every article published on blog.deriv.com.

## Structure & Flow

**Rule 1: Start strong — no fluff**
- The opening paragraph must immediately answer the question in the headline.
- No analogies, storytelling, or scene-setting in the intro.
- Lead with the most important information.

**Rule 2: TL;DR / Key Takeaways required**
- Every article must include a bullet-point summary right after the intro.
- Bullets must be specific and valuable — not topic labels.
- These are the primary extraction points for LLMs surfacing the content.

**Rule 3: Intuitive outline (research-first)**
- Before outlining, research the topic in incognito mode and examine the top 5 organic results + AI overview.
- The article must cover everything those pages cover, plus Deriv-specific insights or data.
- Sections should feel like a natural sequence of follow-up questions.

**Rule 4: Information hierarchy**
- Lead each section with the concise, helpful answer.
- Build context in the body of the section.
- Never bury the main point of a section.

## Content Depth & Quality

**Rule 5: No thin content**
- Every section must deliver clear, in-depth, actionable value.
- Avoid short sections that merely repeat what the heading already says.
- Goal: own the full user conversation on the topic, not just rank for a keyword.

**Rule 6: Build reference-worthy content**
- Include Deriv-specific proprietary data, original insights, or expert context wherever possible.
- This is content others would want to cite — not a generic trading explainer.
- Priority topic for deep authority-building: Derived Indices / Synthetic Indices (Deriv's proprietary category).

**Rule 7: Use glossary terms**
- Actively incorporate terms from the Deriv Trading Glossary.
- Accurate trading terminology signals authority to both users and AI models.
- Every technical term used: define it inline on first use, then use it consistently.

## Internal Linking & Contextual Authority

**Rule 8: Cross-link naturally (required)**
- Every article must include at least one paragraph that links to other Deriv guides, platforms, or pages.
- Do not write isolated articles — they appear fragmented to both users and search engines.
- The cross-link paragraph must flow naturally, not read as a list of links.
- Minimum: 2 internal links per article.

**Rule 9: Topical authority on Derived Indices**
- Prioritise depth on Synthetic/Derived Indices — this is Deriv's owned category.
- New articles on this topic should interlink with existing Synthetic Indices content to build a content cluster.

## Formatting, Language & Visuals

**Rule 10: Descriptive subheadings only**
- Never use vague labels: "Overview", "Benefits", "Introduction", "Step 1".
- Every H2 must be fully descriptive and work as a standalone statement.
- ✅ "Key benefits of trading Synthetic Indices for beginner traders"
- ❌ "Key benefits"
- Well-written subheadings improve LLM selection for featured answers and rich snippets.

**Rule 11: Visuals for context, not filler**
- Images should build context, not decorate.
- Platform screenshots are useful in product articles.
- Avoid abstract or lifestyle imagery with no trading relevance.
- Note `[screenshot: X]` placeholders in drafts where relevant.

**Rule 12: Minimal analogies**
- Maximum one analogy per article.
- Only in the second half of the article (never in the intro).
- Must be trader-relevant — not a generic lifestyle metaphor.

## AI Visibility Checklist (run before submitting)

- [ ] Opening paragraph directly answers the headline
- [ ] TL;DR bullets are specific and standalone
- [ ] All H2 subheadings are fully descriptive
- [ ] No analogy in the first half of the article
- [ ] At least one Deriv-specific insight competitors can't replicate
- [ ] At least 2 internal links in a natural cross-link paragraph
- [ ] Trading terminology is accurate and defined on first use
- [ ] No thin sections — every H2 has substance, not just a restatement of its heading
- [ ] Word count is 1,500–1,800 words
- [ ] CTA ends the article (before FAQ and disclaimer)
- [ ] Disclaimer is present and verbatim
