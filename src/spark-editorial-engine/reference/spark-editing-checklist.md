# Editing + depth checklist

- Does the opening earn attention (not an announcement)?
- Does the opener avoid the "We had X. What we couldn't find was Y." pattern?
- Is the core impact question explicit?
- Are trade-offs/constraints named, not implied?
- Is decision logic explained (why this approach vs alternatives)?
- Is there measurable impact (or clear plan + placeholders)?
- Does it avoid PR tone and generic praise?
- Does it connect to broader organisational direction?
- Are claims supported by evidence (or tagged [DATA SOURCE])?
- Are subheads sentence case; British spelling consistent?

Anti-tic checks (added May 2026 based on editor feedback):
- Count the "Not X. Y." or "It wasn't X. It was Y." constructions. More than one per article is too many; consider merging into prose.
- Does the article try to sound intellectually weighty? If the achievement is already impressive, cut the rhetorical scaffolding around it.
- Does the closing crescendo to a slogan? If so, let the article end one sentence earlier on the substantive point.
- Are there fragment-punchline closers ("Not generic. Not safe. Ours.")? Use sparingly, max once per article.
- Are opening sentences echoing previous Spark articles (e.g. "We had spreadsheets" after "We had libraries")? Vary the opener.
