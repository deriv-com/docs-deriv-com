# Spark voice and tone

Spark is a strategic internal magazine for senior leadership and technical audiences.

Non-negotiables:
- Interpretation over description: explain impact, not updates.
- Surface tensions, trade-offs, constraints, and decision logic.
- Connect local initiatives to broader organisational direction.
- British spelling; sentence case subheadings.
- Avoid corporate clichés / internal PR tone / celebration posts.

Openings:
- Avoid "We're excited to announce…"
- Avoid the "We had X. What we couldn't find was Y." opener pattern. It worked once in Aswathi's "Beyond the last click" but has become a Spark tic. Editor feedback (May 2026): "Is it intentional to start these articles the same way?"
- Prefer one of: a concrete operational pain (specific, visceral, named), a friction/trade-off the reader didn't see coming, a counterintuitive insight, or a verified metric.

Style:
- Precise, lean sentences. Minimal hype.
- If evidence is missing, use placeholders and call out what to confirm.

The parallel-construction tic (avoid):
Editor feedback (May 2026): "Avoid these parallel constructions. They become old fast." Spark drafts have started over-using a particular rhetorical pattern that sounds incisive but is mostly decorative. Examples to avoid:
- "The problem wasn't effort. It was architecture."
- "Not as a feature. As leverage."
- "We weren't lacking insight. We were lacking infrastructure."
- "The ambition isn't comparison. It's proactive positioning."
- "Not just visibility. Simulation."
- "Not creativity. Credentials."
- "No plugin ecosystem. No mastering suite."

Why this fails: the pattern (negation + assertion in short clipped sentences) reads as intellectually weighty on first encounter but adds no information. The actual achievement in the article is usually impressive enough without it. Editor's exact note: "The article spends too much time trying to sound intellectually weighty. The irony is that the actual achievement is already impressive enough without the rhetorical dressing."

When tempted to use this pattern, ask:
1. Does this sentence add information, or just rhythm?
2. Could the same point land as a flowing clause inside the previous sentence?
3. Is this the third or fourth time I've used this structure in the same piece? (One per article, max — and only if it genuinely earns its place.)

The fix is usually to merge the parallel into prose, or to cut it entirely. Examples:
- "The problem wasn't effort. It was architecture." becomes "the kind of structural problem that doesn't get fixed by working harder."
- "Not as a feature. As leverage." becomes "leverage, not a product feature."
- "No plugin ecosystem. No mastering suite." becomes "no plugin ecosystem, no mastering suite" (joined with comma into a single clause).

Rhetorical dressing more broadly:
- Avoid closing crescendos that build to a slogan ("Information isn't optional. Structured information is an advantage. And advantage compounds."). Let the article end on the last substantive point.
- Avoid sentence-fragment punchlines used as section closers ("Not generic. Not safe. Ours.") unless used very sparingly.
- The article should end strong on what was achieved, not on a tagline.

Smart Brevity reminder:
Don't waste readers' time. If a sentence isn't adding new information, cut it. The principle Azita keeps returning to: "the actual achievement is already impressive enough without the rhetorical dressing."
