# Commonly used terms

**Log in vs login**
Log in is a verb. E.g. The system will direct you automatically after you log in.
Login can be a noun or an adjective. E.g. Please keep your account login details safe.

**Sign up vs sign-up**
Sign up is a verb. E.g. Sign up for an account now.
Sign-up can be a noun or an adjective. E.g. We will redirect you to the sign-up page.
We do not use signup.

**Deriv real account**
Deriv real account, not real Deriv account.

**Ebook**
Ebook or ebook, not e-book, eBook, or e-Book.

**Email**
Email or email, not e-mail.

**E-wallet**
E-wallet or e-wallet, not ewallet.

**Select vs selected**
Using select as an adjective can cause a lot of confusion, so tread carefully.
The most common definition of select is (of a group of people or things) carefully chosen from a larger number as being the best or most valuable.
The most common definition of selected is something that has been chosen or picked out from a larger number.
Although certain sources allow for select to be used interchangeably with selected, it's not worth the hassle.

**Among vs between**
Among introduces more than two items, while between is only used when describing two items. E.g. among us, between you and me.

**Biometric vs biometrics**
The word biometric is an adjective, meaning it describes a noun (e.g., biometric data, biometric security). Biometrics is a noun that refers to the field of study, the technology, or the unique physical/behavioral characteristics themselves.

**Staff**
Both singular and plural. Never use staffs unless you're referring to the wooden kind.

**Internet vs internet**
It used to be common practice to capitalise references to the Internet. However, a number of mainstream publications have moved to use internet in recent years as internet connectivity has expanded. We use internet in all media.

**Talent vs talents**
Talent, when it means a talented person or talented people, remains talent, whether singular or plural. Talents means natural abilities to do things well.

**Zip code**
Zip code is treated as a regular noun, not an acronym. Use Zip at the start of a sentence. Use zip mid-sentence or in body copy.

- Zip code must be entered to continue.
- Enter your zip code to find local payment agents.

Avoid: ZIP code
