# Grammar and punctuation

## Headlines, headings, titles

Don't use periods in headlines, headings, and titles.

If you're writing an ad, periods can be considered if you're aiming for strong, short statements, clarity, and precision or to separate words distinctively (in lieu of commas).

- Write better. Faster. Together.
- Brilliant. In every way.

## Periods, question marks, exclamation points

Periods go inside quotation marks. Question marks go inside quotation marks if they're part of the quote. Use exclamation points sparingly and never more than one at a time.

Don't use a period for abbreviations written with the word's first and last letter.

- Saint: St
- Doctor: Dr
- Mister: Mr
- Feet: ft
- Limited: Ltd
- Professor: Prof
- Sergeant: Sgt
- Versus: vs

Put a space between numbers and units of measure.

- 200 cm
- 1 kg

Don't use a period with abbreviations for units of measure in the metric system.

- Centimetre: cm
- Metre: m
- Kilogram: kg

## Ampersand

Use only when part of a formal name or title.

- Ogilvy & Mather
- Barnes & Noble

For team or department names and other scenarios, use "and" instead.

- Accounts and Payments
- Terms and conditions

## Comma

Always use a serial comma if you're listing three or more items in a sentence.

- Cats, dogs, and elephants
- Cats, dogs, elephants, and giraffes

Use a comma before a conjunction when it joins two independent clauses.

- After you set a password, withdrawals will be paused for 24 hours, and you'll be logged out of all sessions.
- Your account has been verified, and you can now start trading.

Don't use a comma between two imperative clauses.

- Save your changes and restart the system.
- Upload your ID and confirm your address.

## Colon

A colon can be used to precede a list of items in a sentence, especially when you want to direct attention to the list.

- In headings, always capitalise the first word after the colon.
- For a point on the list, after the list label, always capitalise the first word after the colon. Example:
  - The "wow" moment: One of the speakers, Benny Leong, mentioned an AI tool that fixed an old photo in 30 seconds.
- If two independent clauses are joined with a colon to make a longer sentence, do not capitalise the first word after the colon. Example:
  - Raunak was clear: it's not a mindless usage of AI that will replace what we do.

## Semicolon

Use a semicolon when multiple commas are being used in a series of items.

...Product Design and UX; Compliance and Risk Management; and the Accounts and Payments teams.

## Apostrophe

Use an apostrophe to contract two words.

- I'm
- We're
- She'd

Don't use an apostrophe to pluralise dates and acronyms.

- CFDs, not CFD's
- 1950s, not 1950's

Don't use an apostrophe if it can be read as a compound noun with the same meaning.

Trade on world markets... (not Trade on the world's markets...)

## Single quotation marks

Use to indicate names and titles when necessary and to indicate a quote within a quote.

"The CEO specifically asked, 'What's Deriv Trader's monthly trading volume for October?'" said Dennis.

## Double quotation marks

Use to indicate direct quotes. Never use it to emphasise a certain word or phrase. Exceptions can be made for terms and conditions.

## Ellipses

Use only to indicate omitted words or phrases in quotes. Use three dots in a row with no spaces between each dot.

- If only she had...

## Hyphen

Used to join words to indicate that they have a combined meaning or that they are linked in the grammar of a sentence.

Numbers between twenty-one and ninety-nine should also be hyphenated when they're spelt out.

- Malaysia-Indonesia border
- First-time trader
- Ten-year-old technology
- To-do list
- Highly acclaimed, critically acclaimed, nationally known (don't hyphenate if the first word is the adverb, 'very', or an adverb that ends in -ly)

## En dash

Used between figures (like dates and times) to show a range.

- 1995–2001
- The shop is open 9:00 am–5:00 pm

## Em dash

Em dashes set off extra information, mark a break in a sentence, or draw attention to a specific point. It's ok to use spaces on both sides of the em dash or use no spaces.

- The team, led by their captain—a seasoned veteran—advanced to the finals.
- The reason for her absence was clear—she was ill.
- The most important thing — the key to success — is perseverance.

## Formulas

If you're writing a formula that requires the multiplication sign, use the actual character instead of a lowercase X. In Google Docs, you can do this by going to **Insert > Special characters**. Use a space before and after a special character (addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division) in the formula.

## Capitalisation

We use the sentence case throughout our apps, websites, and any form of communication with the outside world. Always capitalise the first word in a sentence and capitalise proper nouns.

Page names in a navigation menu within a user interface, whether on web or mobile, should use sentence case. For example:

- Go to your **Profile settings** to change your photo.
- See the current status of your transfer on the **Transfer status** page.

**Exception:** We use lowercase on the sign-up screen when the word "or" acts as a separator between two sign-up or log-in options (e.g., "Sign up using A **or** B").

Book titles are put in the title case (all words are capitalised except for articles and prepositions).

For legal documents, like terms and conditions, terms that are defined can be capitalised.

### Payment agents

Capitalisation rules for "Payment agents" are as follows:

- Capitalised in:
  - The terms & conditions (where it's defined as a specific term)
  - The name of a Wallet (also when referring to plural 'Wallets')
- Sentence case (e.g., "Payment agents") in all other contexts.

### Wallet names with currencies

**Rule:** When a currency name is combined with "Wallet," the currency stays lowercase (per the standard currency rule) but "Wallet" always keeps its capital W, because it's a Deriv product name, not a common noun.

ISO currency codes are the exception; they're always written in full caps, exactly as issued (USD, EUR, GBP) — never partially lowercased.

**Pattern:** [currency name, lowercase] + Wallet / [ISO code, all caps] + Wallet

| Context | ✅ Correct | ❌ Incorrect |
|---|---|---|
| Referring to the main fiat wallet | US dollar Wallet | US Dollar Wallet / US dollar wallet |
| Referring to the P2P-specific wallet | P2P US dollar Wallet | P2P US Dollar Wallet / P2P Wallet |
| Another currency | euro Wallet | Euro Wallet / euro wallet |
| Using the ISO code instead (tight spaces) | USD Wallet | USd Wallet / usd Wallet |
| Mid-sentence, balance reference | Top up your US dollar Wallet balance | Top up your US Dollar Wallet Balance |
| Notification copy | Your P2P US dollar Wallet balance is zero. | Your P2P Wallet balance is zero. |

**Why this rule exists:** Two capitalisation rules intersect here, and it's easy to accidentally apply the wrong one to the whole phrase:

1. Currency names are common nouns → lowercase ("US dollar," not "US Dollar").
2. "Wallet" is a Deriv product name → always capitalised, even mid-sentence.

Writers sometimes capitalise the whole term as if it were one proper noun ("US Dollar Wallet") or lowercase the whole thing as if it were one common phrase ("us dollar wallet"). Neither is right — each half follows its own rule independently.

### Capitalisation after a colon

The word after a colon is usually capitalised, whether what follows is a complete sentence or not. For example:

- Reminder: Your feedback stays anonymous.
- Who will benefit: Employees, clients, partners

**Exception:** If what follows a colon is a parenthetical phrase/list that is part of the sentence, we won't capitalise it. These are cases where an em dash can easily replace a colon. For example:

Jan needed a few more items to complete her job application: a résumé, cover letter, and references.

## Date

Acceptable formats:

- DD Mmm YYYY (e.g. 22 Jan 2025)
- DD Month YYYY (e.g. 22 January 2025)
- DD/MM/YYYY (e.g. 22/01/2025) — Note: since the American system switches the order of month and day, leading with this format is never a good idea and can cause confusion, especially when both the month and the day are below 13.

When it's clear which year the date refers to, you can omit the year:

- DD Mmm (e.g. 22 Jan)
- DD Month (e.g. 22 January)
- DD/MM

It's not necessary to keep the date format consistent within the same document. For example, you can write:

> On Saturday, 22 Jan, there will be system maintenance for up to two hours. There might be another one on 25/02/2025.

**What to avoid:** Don't add "st", "nd", or "th" after the date; don't write 1st Feb, 22nd Jan, 13th Mar.

**Is it ok to write the month in full form?** Yes, if there is no character limit, e.g. when you're writing emails, blog posts, and the like, and you feel the full form gives a more natural feeling and sits organically in your sentence, go with it.

## Time

Use the 12-hour/am/pm format. Use numerals followed by a space and am or pm. If the hour is a single digit, use the H:MM format; if it's two digits, use HH:MM. Use an en dash between times to indicate a time period.

As we're writing for a global audience, use Greenwich Mean Time as the default time and include its abbreviation in parentheses.

- 9:00 am (GMT)
- 9:30 am (GMT)
- 9:30 am–10:30 am (GMT)
- 11:30 am–1:00 pm (GMT)

### Date and time

To combine date and time, use the following sequence:

- 3:20 pm (GMT) on Friday, 11 October 2019
- 3:20 pm (GMT) on 11 Oct 2019
- 3:20 pm (GMT), 11 Oct 2019
- 3:20 pm (GMT), Friday, 11 October 2019

## Lists

- Introduce a list with a colon only if the lead-in statement is a complete sentence.
- Don't use a punctuation mark if the lead-in statement is a sentence fragment.
- Start each list item with a capital letter.
- Don't use a punctuation mark after the list items that are not complete sentences.
- Use normal terminal punctuation (period, question mark, or exclamation point) after the list items that are complete sentences (including imperative sentences).

## Names and titles

Capitalise the names of departments and teams (but not the word "team" or "department").

- Join the Marketing team...
- Our IT department…
- Our Front-end Development team…
- Customer Support team

Capitalise individual job titles if referencing a specific role, but don't capitalise if referencing in general terms.

- The new Marketing Director starts today.
- All the managers ate durian.

Capitalise academic degrees only when the full name of the degree is used, such as Bachelor of Arts or Master of Social Work. General references, such as bachelor's, master's or doctoral degree, are not capitalised. Do not capitalise graduate certificates. Names of academic disciplines, majors, minors, programmes, and courses of study are not proper nouns and should not be capitalised except for languages such as English, French, and Japanese. Use an apostrophe with bachelor's degree and master's degree, but not in Bachelor of Arts or Master of Science. Do not use an apostrophe with associate degree or doctoral degree. Do not use periods in degree abbreviations.

- Bachelor of Arts in literature
- Bachelor of Science in biology
- She graduated with a bachelor's degree in communication. (recommended for Deriv job descriptions)
- She's planning to pursue a master's degree in English. (recommended for Deriv job descriptions)
- She holds a doctoral degree (or doctorate) from Stanford University.

## Currency

Use a specific three-letter currency code to indicate the currency you're referring to.

- 1,000 USD
- 5 EUR
- 5,344.00 GBP
- 70 AUD
- USD account
- 5.7 million USD
- 2.5 BTC

When describing the currency, spell it out fully and don't capitalise.

- You have successfully changed your currency to the euro.
- On the bright side, the pound sterling is not the worst-performing currency in the world today.
- The Australian dollar was hit hard overnight.
- GBP/USD is a currency pair that involves buying the pound sterling and selling the US dollar.
- Market intel suggests that investors should capitalise on the current price dip and buy bitcoin.

Don't spell out the currency in full if describing an account type. Use the abbreviation or three-letter currency code instead.

- You have added a BTC account.
- You do not have any balance remaining in your USDT account.
- You cannot change your account currency because you have deposited money into your USD account.

For cryptocurrencies, capitalise if you're referring to the technology, concept, or units of the currency.

- We still don't know who created Bitcoin.
- Someone sent me 12 Bitcoins by mistake!
- If you want to trade with Litecoin as your account currency, switch to your LTC account.
- Trade cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin, Ether, Ripple, and Litecoin without taking ownership of the underlying.

## Numbers

Spell out a number when it begins a sentence unless it's ridiculously long (whereby you should rephrase the sentence so the number doesn't come at the beginning). Otherwise, use the numeral (including ordinals). An exception can be made for headlines if you're starting the sentence with a number.

- Three new developers will start on Monday, and 5 more next week.
- I bought 3 new pairs of shoes last weekend.
- We won 1st place in the Best Online Trading Provider category, an improvement over our 3rd place finish last year.
- 20 years of experience and trust (headline)

Use commas for numbers over three digits unless they're part of a proper noun or used to describe leverage.

- 345
- 1,200
- 4,637,900
- Leverage up to 1:2000
- Crash 1000 Index

Use the % symbol instead of spelling out "per cent." It's better to use numerals and not the word for percentages, even for numbers less than 10, unless at the beginning of the sentence (but try to rephrase so the number doesn't come at the beginning if possible).

- Over 50% of our clients are from Brazil.
- Due to last week's pricing error, we had to refund 5% of our clients.
- Eighty per cent of retail traders lose their money.

When writing two numbers right next to each other, spell out the first number and use a numeral for the second number to make it easier to read.

- We tested fifty-two 18-inch rims and found the Canary brand to be the best.
- We tested six 18-inch rims and found the Canary brand to be the best.

## Hyperlinks

Avoid using "click here" which hurts usability, accessibility, and SEO. Links should clearly explain where they will take you because you won't have to remember the context if you skim the page. Hyperlink the noun and not the verb phrase.

- For more information, visit the Help centre.

## Abbreviations and acronyms

Spell out the meaning the first time, then use the shortened version after.

- You may trade contracts for difference (CFDs) on the Deriv MetaTrader 5 platform. CFD trading allows you to trade on a wide range of markets, from cryptocurrencies to metals, with up to 1:1000 leverage.
