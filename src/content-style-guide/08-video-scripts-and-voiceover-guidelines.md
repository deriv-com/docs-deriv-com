# Video scripts and voiceover guidelines

When crafting video scripts or voiceover content for Deriv, aim to maintain the same tone and style as the rest of our communications — professional yet approachable, clear, and informative. Since the audience is global, it's essential to use jargon-free language and simple sentences, ensuring that non-native English speakers can understand the message.

The scripts should be engaging and easy to follow, with content centred around the audience's needs and derived from our key tone of voice guidelines: clear and direct, friendly and approachable, client-centred, trustworthy and transparent, informative and engaging.

## Clarity and simplicity

- Keep sentences short and straightforward to ensure quick comprehension.
- Use a conversational tone, avoiding over-complicated language or long sentences.
- Be mindful of a global audience – avoid idiomatic expressions or cultural references that may not translate well.

## Hook the viewer early

- Start with a strong hook to capture the viewer's attention within the first 10 seconds.
- Highlight intriguing or key information early on to spark curiosity, and maintain engagement throughout the script.

## Maintain natural flow

- Write as if speaking directly to the audience. Scripts for voiceovers should feel natural when spoken aloud.
- Use active voice to make the content more engaging and easier to digest.

## Emphasise key messages

- Identify the three main takeaways for your audience and make sure these points are made clearly throughout the video.

## End with a CTA

- Always finish with a call to action (CTA), whether signing up, visiting the website, or contacting customer support. The CTA should be concise, focused, and appropriate for the video's objective.

## Script formatting guidelines

### Script sections

- Visual cues should be formatted in italics and enclosed in square brackets. Example: *[Drone shot of office team interaction]*.
- Dialogue and voiceover text should be plain and follow natural speech patterns.
- Actions or specific notes should be enclosed in parentheses (for example, sound effect, pause, emphasis).

### Instructions for voice talent

- Write emphasis words in bold to ensure special attention is given while delivering.
- Use pauses strategically – indicate where breaks or pauses for viewer comprehension would be effective.
