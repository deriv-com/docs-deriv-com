# General guidelines

## We use British English in all our content

The only exception is when it comes to unique names. For example, the U.S. Department of Labor and Microsoft Media Center.

Common spelling differences between British English and American English:

| UK | US |
|---|---|
| Programme | Program |
| Centre | Center |
| Colour | Color |
| Cheque | Check |
| Analogue | Analog |
| Authorise | Authorize |
| Favourite | Favorite |
| Fulfil | Fulfill |
| Metre | Meter |
| Licence (noun), license (verb) | License (noun and verb) |
| Practice (noun), practise (verb) | Practice (noun and verb) |

## Proofread, proofread, proofread

Proofreading helps readers to focus on your message, not your mistakes.

## Keep it simple and be clear

Use simple, conversational language. Use short, uncomplicated sentences and paragraphs. Avoid jargon at all costs. If you must use a technical term, clearly define it so everyone can understand.

## Know our audience

We produce content that caters to people from around the world with various interests and backgrounds. We should be inclusive and speak the language that our clients use. Write from the perspective of the reader.

## Avoid redundancy

Examples: New innovation, future plans, prior experience, throughout the month of June.

## Avoid ambiguity

Be specific. For example, say, "We'll get back to you in two days" instead of "We'll get back to you soon".

## Avoid using fillers

Avoid using words like "furthermore", "however", "therefore", etc.

## Write using active voice

Active voice is preferred because it makes our writing much easier to understand. It puts the sentence's subject first and performs the action that the rest of the sentence describes.

## Write positively

Use positive language rather than negative language. For example, "Volatility Indices are not available in some countries" can be written as "Volatility Indices are available in selected countries."

## Anglo-Saxon over Latinate (for English)

Use simplified verbs such as "need/give" instead of "require/provide".
