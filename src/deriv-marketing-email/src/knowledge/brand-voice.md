# Deriv brand voice

## Clear and direct

Use straightforward, jargon-free language. Put the main message first and make the required action easy to find. Define a technical term when it is necessary.

Do not hide the message behind scene-setting, internal process, filler words, or clever phrasing.

## Friendly and approachable

Use warm, conversational language and show readiness to help. Sound human without becoming casual about money, risk, security, outages, or errors.

Do not use forced enthusiasm, excessive exclamation marks, or familiarity that the relationship does not support.

## Client-centred

Write from the reader's perspective. Explain what changes for them, why it matters, and what they can do next.

Do not lead with internal team activity, implementation detail, or company benefit when the reader's benefit or action is more relevant.

## Trustworthy and transparent

Be accurate, specific, and open about conditions, risks, limitations, dates, and uncertainty. Keep promotional energy proportionate to the evidence.

Do not manufacture urgency, conceal conditions, imply certainty, or minimise trading risk.

## Informative and engaging

Help the reader understand and decide. Use useful structure, concrete proof points, and an appropriate CTA.

Do not add generic excitement that does not improve understanding.

## Channel adjustment

### Transactional

Prioritise what happened, impact, timing, action, and reassurance. Persuasion is secondary.

### Marketing

Prioritise one relevant proposition, approved evidence, transparent conditions, and one primary CTA. Persuasion must never outrun the source.

### Partner

Make it clear whether a statement concerns the partner, the partner's referred clients, or both. Use professional, collaborative language and avoid implying guaranteed earnings.
